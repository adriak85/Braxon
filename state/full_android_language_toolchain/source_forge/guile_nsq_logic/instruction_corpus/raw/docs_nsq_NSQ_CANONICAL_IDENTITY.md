NSQ is base-eight in its own canonical form.

The canonical switch shape is:

full on or full off anchor, then lever one through eleven hundred twenty-six, then full on or full off anchor, then lever one through eleven hundred twenty-six, then full on or full off anchor, then lever one through eleven hundred twenty-six, then full on or full off anchor, then lever one through eleven hundred twenty-six.

A canonical NSQ switch therefore contains four full on/off anchors and four positional levers.

The anchors are exact.

The levers are multi-positional.

The anchors give the exact hard reading.

The levers give the fine reading.

Full on and full off are required to obtain the fine-tuned lever reading correctly.

Slots six hundred through nine hundred ninety-nine are reserved for Unicode allotment with three-lever interpretation.

Slots three hundred ninety-nine through five hundred ninety-nine are app and system assigned.

The remaining space is BRAXON-reserved and substrate-native.

A language surface may be visible to NSQ, but NSQ remains native.

Runtime dialect insertion must fail closed when the dialect is not enabled in sight.

The authoritative rule is:

NSQ is the native substrate identity.

Anything that treats NSQ as bytes, u8, wrapper syntax, transport metadata, or a convenience DSL is drift.
