# Chapter 1 — The Shattered Song Prophecy

The dream began with water.

Not an ocean as Pip knew oceans, with weather above them and a horizon that could be trusted. This sea had no shore. It rose in every direction, dark violet beneath a sky without stars, and it carried light inside itself as though the water had swallowed a thousand distant moons.

Pip stood on the surface without sinking.

He was twenty-five, though the dream tried to make age feel like a number written on glass: visible, fragile, irrelevant. He knew the weight of adulthood in the way he held himself. He knew the choices he had already made, and the ones he had avoided. The dream did not give him a younger face. It gave him the man he had become and asked him to look forward.

Something was singing beneath the water.

The sound was broken.

A phrase would rise, clear enough to understand, and then vanish before the next phrase could complete it. Pip listened harder. The fragments did not form a song so much as the memory of one.

A voice whispered his name.

He turned.

There was no one there.

Then the sea opened.

Far below, a line of blue light descended through the violet water. It did not illuminate the ocean around it. Instead, everything outside the line became darker, as though the light were cutting a narrow path through some enormous hidden thing.

Pip heard three notes.

The first felt like a warning.

The second felt like grief.

The third felt like a door opening.

The broken song returned, and this time he understood enough to know that it was not predicting a single event. It was warning him about choices: about what could be preserved, what could be lost, and what would become possible only after something precious had been surrendered.

The water beneath his feet moved.

A shape passed under him, enormous and silent.

Pip stepped backward.

The surface did not break.

Instead, the thing beneath the water looked up.

He could not see its face, but he knew the sensation of being recognized. Not by a person. Not by an animal. By something old enough that the difference between remembering and waiting had ceased to matter.

The song broke again.

This time the fragments came faster.

A name.

A wound.

A road.

A choice.

And, beneath all of them, a single instruction that was less a sentence than an instinct:

Do not mistake the first door for the only door.

Pip woke before he could answer.

For several seconds he lay perfectly still.

The room was dark. The ordinary sounds of the Oasis had not yet begun: no footsteps, no voices, no movement beyond the walls. His heart was beating too quickly for a dream that was already disappearing.

He reached for the notebook beside him.

He wrote down the three notes before he could forget them.

Then he stopped.

There was a fourth mark on the page.

He had not written it.

A thin blue line crossed the paper from one edge to the other.

Pip stared at it.

The line pulsed once.

Somewhere beyond the room, Mack shifted.

Pip closed the notebook.

The day had not started yet.

But whatever had been waiting in the dream had already found its way into the waking world.
