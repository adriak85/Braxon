# Chapter 1 — The Amethyst Ocean Dream

The Amethyst Ocean had no horizon.

Pip knew that first.

There was water beneath him, water stretching farther than sight could measure, and above it a violet sky so deep that the distinction between sea and sky had almost disappeared. The ocean was not merely purple. It seemed to contain purple light of its own, a slow amethyst radiance moving through the depths like breath.

He stood upon it.

He did not ask why.

Dreams had their own rules, and this one felt less like invention than arrival.

The water was perfectly still until something moved beneath it.

Pip looked down.

The movement was too large to understand. It crossed beneath his feet without breaking the surface, a presence rather than a creature, and left behind a faint disturbance of light.

Then he heard the song.

It came from everywhere.

Not a voice. Not exactly music. It was a pattern carried through the ocean itself, rising and falling in fragments. Pip caught pieces of it without being able to hold the whole thing in his mind.

A beginning.

A separation.

A wound.

A choice.

A return.

Then silence.

The song began again.

This time Pip understood that it was broken because something had broken it.

He turned slowly in the empty ocean.

There was no shore. No vessel. No person waiting for him.

Only the amethyst water and the sound beneath it.

A thin blue radiance appeared in the distance.

Pip watched it approach.

It was not coming through the air. It was rising from the ocean floor, straight upward, a narrow line of impossible blue that divided the water without disturbing it.

The song changed when the light appeared.

Pip felt something inside himself tighten.

He knew, without knowing how, that this was not a warning about what would happen to him.

It was a warning about what he would choose.

The distinction mattered.

A choice could be survived.

A consequence could not always be undone.

The ocean darkened.

For one instant Pip saw images beneath its surface—not a sequence he could name, but impressions of lives touching, separating, returning, and carrying wounds forward. He saw Mack without seeing a human face. He felt the weight of the Oasis. He felt the pressure of a path not yet taken.

Then came the prophecy.

Not spoken aloud.

It arrived as certainty.

The song had shattered, but its pieces still belonged to one song.

What was broken could become a map.

What was chosen would become a world.

Pip reached toward the blue line.

The ocean moved beneath him.

Something vast passed below.

He stopped.

The presence below did not attack him. It waited.

That waiting frightened him more than violence would have.

Because it felt as though the ocean already knew him.

Pip lowered his hand.

The blue light vanished.

The song stopped.

For a moment there was nothing.

Then one final note crossed the Amethyst Ocean.

It was quiet.

It was whole.

And Pip understood the only part of the prophecy that the dream was willing to give him:

The first choice would not decide everything.

It would decide what became possible next.

Pip woke before dawn.

He remained still, listening.

The room around him was real. The darkness was ordinary. His breathing slowed.

He was an adult man in the Oasis, twenty-five years old, with a life already made from decisions and consequences. Whatever the dream had been, it had not returned him to childhood. It had brought him forward.

He reached for the notebook beside him.

Before writing anything, he tried to remember the song.

He could not.

He remembered only the feeling of something shattered that was still, somehow, one thing.

Pip opened the notebook.

A faint blue line was already waiting on the page.

He stared at it.

Outside, something shifted through the Oasis.

Mack.

Pip did not call out.

He simply held the notebook open and watched the blue line pulse once.

The dream was over.

Whatever it had begun was not.
